1

Discount badges read “(–2547%)”

A shopper's first glance lands on broken math. Broken math reads as “what else on this site is broken?”

What we found

Your product cards and search results compute discount % from compare-at prices that were never cleaned up during import — values like ₹1,424,900 on a ₹10,560 phone. The badge prints the negative result instead of hiding it.

data-compare="1424900" ×4  ·  data-compare="39900" ×4  ·  data-compare="14900" ×4
badge text: "(--2547%)" ×4  ·  "(--3793%)" ×4  ·  "(-0%)" ×12

Why it costs sales

Used-phone buyers are already risk-sensitive. A store that can't do percentage arithmetic invites the question they fear most: “is this unit what it says it is?”

The fix

Strip corrupt compare-at values, or show a badge only when the math is real. On the concept PDP we rebuilt for your iPhone 7: one honest price, no fake MRP, no percentage at all — see it here.

Trust leak · affects every product card
2

Warranty says three different numbers

6 months in the top bar. 12 months on the cards. “Life Time Warranty” four more times in the page source.

What we found

One page of fonezone.com carries all three claims simultaneously — in the announcement bar, the product-card badges, and embedded section text. A buyer comparing two phones can't tell which promise applies to which unit.

"Upto 6 months warranty" ×1 (top bar, every page)
"12 Months Warranty" ×14 (product cards)
"Life Time Warranty" ×4 (section copy)

Why it costs sales

Warranty is the #1 objection in used-phone retail. Contradiction turns an objection into a support ticket — or worse, a chargeback when the invoice says something the banner didn't.

The fix

One number, everywhere: banner, card, PDP, invoice, WhatsApp receipt. If a unit genuinely carries a different term, say it on that unit — not as a second global claim. We used your own header's number (6 months) throughout the concept PDP.

Support + chargeback risk · every page
3

Decimal prices + “Part Change” jargon in titles

₹17,560.33 and “Apple iPhone 12 mini 128GB Green Good (Part Change…” — words your customer doesn't know, in the one line they always read.

What we found

Prices render to two decimals site-wide (₹10,560.39, ₹25,400.37, ₹17,560.33). Product titles include internal grading notes — “(Part Change Message)” appears 30 times in site source — and truncate mid-phrase on cards.

"Part Change Message" ×30 in site source
"Rs. 10,560.39" · "Rs. 17,560.33" · "Rs. 25,400.37"

Why it costs sales

Paise on a phone reads like an FX glitch, not a price. “Part Change” is your internal grading language leaking into the storefront — shoppers skip what they can't parse, and truncated titles kill the comparison shopping that drives AOV.

The fix

Round to ₹. Translate internal notes into the grade system buyers already understand (A / B / C with disclosed flaws) — which is exactly what the concept PDP does, using your real product photos.
